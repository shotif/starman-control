## 1. Where M4 starts

- **Five powers deal with each other** (M3): contact from a signal to talk, regard with its
  reasons, treaties (open lanes, shared research, borders, trade), exchanges, and the Hollow Wake's
  passage, bribes and protection. Protection is paid but does nothing yet: M3 promised that from
  M4 *the seller's ships leave the buyer's systems alone* (M3_NEIGHBOURS.md §23.4).
- **There are no warships.** Every ship is a survey, outpost or colony ship, ordered one at a time
  (M1). Ships pay 1 credit of upkeep a turn. Ships use their own power's lanes, and a partner's
  with open lanes or passage; only survey ships jump blind.
- **Nothing can be lost:** a colony in revolt stops but is never lost (D-35), and no game ends.
  The score (D-50) ranks the powers with no victory; D-14 (victory conditions) was left for M4.
- **The research tree** has six fields (Propulsion, Construction, Life Sciences, Society, Survey,
  Xenology) and 62 technologies. SCOPE §4.6 planned an *Energy and Weapons* field that was never
  built, since nothing needed it.
- **At turn 100 of the headless games** (M2_BALANCE.md) each power holds 6 to 16 colonies on
  average, 40 to 100 million people, and the rivals keep 40 to 230 credits.
- **The owner's try of M3** (100 turns or more, on a phone and a desktop) was asked for; what they
  find is folded in here when it comes.

## 2. Goals

- **Battles and wars** (D-58): powers build warships, gather them into fleets, go to war and make
  peace, fight battles that the player shapes with orders beforehand and can watch afterwards
  (D-07), and take each other's worlds.
- **Every battle explains itself:** who fought, what was lost, and why it went that way.
- **Designs that matter:** what a power researches and builds decides how its ships fight, and a
  counter can be found to an enemy's designs.
- **Wars with reasons and a price:** declared, remembered by everyone, paid for in upkeep and in
  weariness, and ended by peace or by surrender.
- **A game that ends:** a power can win by conquest or by the council's vote, and the player can
  lose (D-14).
- **The rivals fight by their character**, through the same orders as the player's (D-08), and the
  outlaw raids as M3 promised.
- **Phone and desktop alike** (D-04): every new screen on both, and the replay watchable on a phone.

## 3. Out of scope

Admirals and other named leaders (M5, SCOPE §4.14); espionage (M5); relics, the keys and the
victory they give (M5, D-13); carriers and fighters, stations that move, very large hulls (later,
SCOPE §4.7); tactical command of a battle (later, D-07); playing an alien power (later, D-08);
more species (M5).

## 4. Warships and their designs (D-59)

SCOPE §4.7: hulls from small to capital, a designer with slots for weapons, shields, armour, drives
and special modules, good designs offered automatically, and refits.

- **A. Hulls and modules, with the yard's designs and refits (recommended).** Five warship hulls,
  each with room for more modules: the **corvette** (2), **frigate** (3), **destroyer** (4),
  **cruiser** (6) and **capital ship** (8); and a sixth civil hull, the **troop transport** (§10).
  A module is a **weapon** (one of Reborn's four damage types: energy, kinetic, plasma, ion), a
  **shield** (Reborn's balanced, deflector or diffuser), **armour**, or a **special** (a targeting
  computer, a repair bay, a drive that adds a jump point), each in marks from research. A
  **design** is a hull with a module in each place, named. Each power's yard offers **its design
  for every hull** it can build, made from the best it knows and renewed when research brings
  better; the player may make and name their own, and the rivals use the yard's. A **refit** at a
  shipyard brings a ship to its hull's newest design for the difference in cost, in a turn. Each
  power's shipmakers name its warships and give them their look (§8); the rules are the same for
  every power.
- **B. Fixed classes, no designer.** Each hull comes in one design that research improves. The
  least to learn and to build, but nothing to counter an enemy with but numbers.
- **C. A full designer** in the manner of SCOPE's inspiration: many module kinds and sizes, power
  and space to balance, facings. Much to show on a phone, and a long increment.

## 5. The Weapons field (D-60)

- **A. A seventh field, Weapons (recommended)**, about 20 technologies over five tiers: weapon
  marks for each damage type, the three shields and their marks, armour, the destroyer, cruiser and
  capital hulls, defences (batteries, the planetary shield, barracks), troop transports and repair
  bays. It costs as the other fields do (with M2b's rising costs), so a power that arms learns less
  of the rest. Every power starts able to build corvettes and frigates with energy and kinetic
  guns, balanced shields and plate armour, so a war can be fought from the first turn.
- **B. Spread over the fields there are:** hulls in Construction, drives in Propulsion, shields in
  Energy… There is no Energy field, and a warlike power's choices would be hidden among the rest.

## 6. Fleets and their orders (D-61)

D-07 decided that *each task force's stance, target priority and retreat point are set before the
battle*. SCOPE §4.7 made fleets of task forces, each with a role (line, escort, screen, support,
transport).

- **A. Fleets with their own orders; fleets in one battle fight as task forces (recommended).** A
  **fleet** is ships of one power together in a system, named, ordered as one: it moves along lanes
  at the pace of its slowest ship, and it has a **stance** (engage, defend, evade), a **target
  priority** (warships, the largest, the smallest, transports) and a **retreat point** (when it has
  lost a quarter, half, three quarters, or never). Fleets are formed, merged and split at will;
  civil ships may sail with them (a colony ship under escort, a troop convoy). When several fleets
  of one power are in one battle, each fights as **a task force with its own orders**, so the
  player who wants a screen and a line of battle makes two fleets. One list of fleets is all a
  phone has to show.
- **B. Task forces inside each fleet,** each with a role and its own orders. As SCOPE drafted, but
  three to five sets of orders for every fleet before every battle, and a deeper sheet on a phone.
- **C. No fleets:** every ship ordered alone, as now. Simplest to build; at war, tens of ships to
  move one by one.

**What limits fleets**, under any option: **upkeep by hull size** (a corvette 1 credit a turn, a
capital ship 5), so fleets follow the economy (SCOPE §4.7's command limit is left out: upkeep does
its work with one number fewer); **damage that stays** after a battle; and **repairs** only near
home: quickly at a shipyard, slowly in a system with one's own colony, not at all elsewhere.

## 7. Battles (D-62)

**Where.** A battle is fought in a system where, after the turn's movement, armed ships or
defences of two powers hostile to each other are present: powers at war (§9), or a raider and the
power it raids (§11). A fleet that enters a system where a hostile power has armed ships or
defences **stops there** (it is intercepted), so lanes, chokepoints and the systems that guard them
matter (D-19).

- **A. Rounds at three ranges (recommended).** Up to eight rounds. Each task force closes to or
  holds the range its stance seeks (*engage*: close; *defend*: middle, where it stands; *evade*:
  long, leaving when it can). Each weapon has its best range (kinetic long, energy and ion middle,
  plasma close) and does less outside it. Each ship fires at targets its task force's priority
  chooses; hits are rolled from the game's seed, so the same game and the same orders give the same
  battle (D-06). Damage meets shields first, by Reborn's damage matrix (ion breaks shields and
  barely scratches a hull; deflectors shed kinetic slugs; diffusers spread energy), and shields
  recover a share each round; then armour, then the hull. A task force that has lost its retreat
  point leaves for its nearest own system. Defences fight as a task force of their own. Every
  battle is **reported**: the forces, the losses, the rounds in brief, and **why** ("their
  deflectors shed our railguns; plasma would burn through").
- **B. One comparison of strength.** Each side's strength, losses by the ratio, a line in the
  report. Quick, but little to plan and nothing to watch.

## 8. The replay (D-63)

D-07 decided *a short 3D replay to watch, skip or rewind*. How:

- **A. In 3D, in the system where it was fought (recommended).** The system's star and worlds as the
  system view draws them (M1, D-25), and the ships drawn procedurally in each power's look and
  colour, from Reborn's ship kit (D-09, D-10): the Authority's balanced Halden and armoured Ares
  hulls, the Cooperative's frugal, fast and cheap makers', the Wake's salvaged hulls rebuilt, and a
  look of their own for the Duskfolk's and the Drifters' ships (fiction). Shots, shields flaring
  and ships breaking up, round by round, in about 30 seconds; play, pause, a round at a time,
  rewind, faster, and *skip*; the report beside it. On a phone, full screen with the controls in
  thumb reach. No pictures to buy.
- **B. A 2D schematic:** the task forces in lines at their ranges, shots as lines between them.
  Lighter and clearer on a small phone, less to see.
- **C. The report only in M4,** the replay later.

## 9. War and peace (D-64)

D-53 left non-aggression, alliance and war for M4.

- **A. Declared war, peace, pacts and capitulation (recommended).**
  - **War is declared** (an order). It ends every treaty between the two at once and is remembered:
    by the other (*declared war on us*), and less by every power in contact with the declarer, more
    by the lawful.
  - **At war** fleets fight where they meet; warships may use the enemy's open lanes (*forced
    passage*), stopped by its fleets and defences; colonies may be invaded and bombarded (§10).
  - **Peace** is offered and answered as a treaty: each keeps what it holds, an exchange may go
    with it (M3's), and neither may declare war on the other for 10 turns.
  - **Non-aggression** (a treaty): to declare war it must first be broken (M3's breach: −20, heard
    by all). **Alliance** (a treaty): open lanes, each sees the other's ships, and each is asked
    to join a war declared on the other (the rivals join when warm).
  - **Capitulation:** a power at war may surrender to another. It keeps what it holds but is
    **subdued**: it pays a fifth of its taxes as tribute, cannot declare war, and is at war with
    whoever its victor fights. A subdued power counts as beaten for conquest (§13). The rivals
    surrender when they have lost their capital or most of their colonies.
  - **War weariness:** each turn at war, and each ship and colony lost, costs approval at every
    colony, a little at first and more as the war goes on, by each power's character (the
    Cooperative tires fastest). Peace lets it fade.
- **B. War and peace only.** No pacts, no alliances, no surrender: conquest then means taking every
  colony of every rival, across 33 light-years.

## 10. Defences and invasion (D-65)

SCOPE §4.8: planetary shields, batteries and garrisons built as projects; troop transports landing
armies; resolved as radio traffic from the commanders to Control; bombardment and blockade.

- **A. All of SCOPE §4.8 (recommended).**
  - **Defences** are projects: the **defence battery** (guns that fight in any battle at its
    system, with its power's best weapon), the **planetary shield** (stops bombardment and shields
    the batteries) and **barracks** (a larger garrison). Each is a building in the colony view.
  - **Garrison:** every colony has troops from its people, and more with barracks; an outpost has
    none.
  - **Troop transports** (a civil hull) carry troops raised at the colony that builds them.
  - **Invasion:** transports in a system where the enemy has no armed ships or defences left land
    their troops on a colony as the turn ends; troops and garrison fight, rolled from the seed. If
    the invaders win, the colony is theirs, with its people (unhappy for a while) and buildings; a
    capital taken moves its power's capital to its largest colony. An outpost falls to any troops.
    Each landing is told as **radio traffic**, the commanders' words to Control.
  - **Bombardment** (an order): a fleet holding a system's orbit kills people and wrecks buildings
    each turn, unless a planetary shield stands; every power that hears of it remembers.
  - **Blockade:** a colony whose orbit a hostile power holds is cut off from its power's food and
    trade, and makes less.
- **B. Defences and invasion only;** no bombardment or blockade.

## 11. Raids and protection (D-66)

M3 sold protection from the Wake's ships before they could do anything (§23.4).

- **A. The outlaw raids without war (recommended).** A power that is not lawful (the Hollow Wake)
  may order a fleet to **raid** a system of a power it is not at war with: it fights that power's
  armed ships and defences there, and if it holds the orbit it takes **plunder** each turn
  (credits from a colony, a resource from an outpost). A raid is remembered by the raided and by
  every lawful power that hears of it, but it is not war: no invasion, no bombardment. **Protection
  is kept:** a power that sells protection may not raid its buyers, and the Wake as a rival raids
  those near it who do not pay.
- **B. Raids only in war.** The Wake is like any other power, and protection buys nothing but
  regard.

## 12. The council (D-67)

SCOPE §4.9: the council forms once enough powers know each other; its members vote on resolutions
and on who leads it; elected leader by a large majority is a victory (D-14). Its name and rules are
to be designed.

- **A. Elections and two resolutions (recommended).** **The Assembly** (a working name, fiction)
  forms the first time three powers can each talk with the other two; any power that can talk with
  a member joins. Each member has **a vote for every million of its people**, as SCOPE's
  inspiration weighed its senate by population. Every 20 turns (five years) it **elects a leader**:
  the two members with the most votes stand, and every member votes for one or abstains (the
  rivals by their regard). **A candidate with two thirds of all the members' votes is elected and
  wins the game.** Between elections a member may propose one of two **resolutions**: *sanctions*
  on a member at war with another (every member ends its trade with it), or *a call for peace* to
  two members at war (sanctions on whichever refuses peace within 5 turns). A member's vote follows
  its regard. A subdued power votes as its victor does.
- **B. Elections only:** the victory path, with nothing for the Assembly to do between elections.
- **C. More resolutions:** A, and lane law, embargoes, expelling a member, joint fleets. Later, if
  the Assembly is too quiet.

## 13. Victory and defeat (D-14)

D-14 recommended *conquest, a council vote, the artefact hunt, and a score at a turn limit*. In
M4:

- **A. Conquest, the Assembly's vote, and a score at a turn limit (recommended).**
  - **Conquest:** every other power in the game has lost its last colony or has capitulated to
    you.
  - **The Assembly's choice:** elected its leader by two thirds of the votes (§12).
  - **At a turn limit** (a new-game setting: 150, 200 or 250 turns, or none; 250 by default, the
    long end of D-16's game), the highest score (D-50) wins.
  - **The keys** come with M5.
  - **Defeat:** your last colony lost, your capitulation, or another power's victory. A lost power
    leaves the game; its outposts and ships go with it, and its lanes go dark.
  - **The end:** a screen of victory or defeat with the story of the game in numbers; after a
    victory the game can be played on.
- **B. Conquest and the vote, with no turn limit.** A game goes on until someone wins it.

## 14. Rivals at war (D-68)

- **A. By character (recommended)**, through the same orders as the player's, and always for a
  reason the player can read (a hostile regard, a claim crossed, an outlaw's raids, an ally's call):
  - **The Transit Authority** (law) keeps a fleet at home and declares war only on a power it holds
    hostile or one that raids it; it makes peace once its losses mount.
  - **The Frontier Co-op** (peace) never declares war, defends, joins alliances, and is the first to
    make peace.
  - **The Hollow Wake** (outlaw) raids those near it who do not buy protection, declares war on a
    weaker neighbour it regards coldly, and sells peace for credits.
  - **The Dusk Concord** (slow to forget) declares war on a power that keeps settling in its claim,
    when it is the stronger, and is slow to make peace.
  - **The Drift Moot** (brokers) never declares war, buys protection, and pays for peace.
  - Every rival arms when a neighbour arms (the Watch sees it), defends its colonies, invades where
    it is stronger, and surrenders when it has lost its capital or most of its colonies.
- **B. Strength alone:** every rival declares war on whoever is weaker. More wars, fewer reasons.
- **C. No rival starts a war:** they defend, and the Wake still raids. A gentle game; conquest then
  is only the player's.

The headless games show how often wars come and who wins (§18).

## 15. Words from the other powers (D-17)

D-17 asked whether text should be written live by a language model while playing (for example,
alien envoys speaking): declarations of war, offers of peace and radio traffic make the question
real.

- **A. Every line written into the game (recommended).** As every line is now, in each power's own
  voice, with variants chosen by the seed. A public static site cannot hold an API key (D-09), and
  every line can be read and checked before it ships.
- **B. Lines written at build time** with Gemini, reviewed and committed like the pictures: more
  variety for a few cents, and the same checks.
- **C. The player's own key** in their browser, for live words from the envoys: possible on a
  static site, but the words cannot be checked, need the network, and are out of scope for 1.0
  (SCOPE §5).

## 16. On the screen

- **Fleets on the map:** one mark for a fleet, with its number of ships and its power's colour and
  shape; a hostile fleet in sight marked as such. Selecting a fleet shows its card: its ships,
  strength, damage and orders, with *Move*, *Stance*, *Target*, *Retreat*, *Split*, *Merge*,
  *Raid*, *Invade* and *Bombard* where they apply. On a phone the Empire's *Ships* tab becomes
  *Fleets*; on a desktop a fleets panel with **F**.
- **The designer:** from a shipyard's queue and the Empire. On a phone, a sheet for each design (its
  hull, then each place and its module as a list, with what the design does in numbers); on a
  desktop, the hull's places beside the modules, with two designs compared.
- **Battles in the report,** each with *Watch*, which opens the replay (§8); *what needs you*: a
  fleet that retreated, a colony under blockade or siege, an offer of peace, an election.
- **War and peace on the diplomacy screen:** *Declare war*, with what it will cost said first; peace,
  non-aggression, alliance and capitulation among the treaties.
- **The Assembly:** a screen of its own with its members, their votes, the next election and the
  resolutions; from the menu and the Powers tab on a phone, **A** on a desktop.
- **The Admiral:** an eighth advisor for each power, the voice of its fleets in the report, with a
  face (3 portraits for the human powers).
- **The end:** a screen of victory or defeat.

## 21. Increment 41 in detail: warships (written before building it)

**Goals.** D-59 and D-60 as decided (A): five warship hulls, modules for each place, designs made
from them, the yard's design for every hull a power can build, renewed as research brings better,
the player's own designs, refits at a shipyard, and the Weapons field's technologies for hulls,
weapons, shields, armour and specials. Upkeep by hull size (D-61). The Admiral, an eighth advisor,
speaks for the fleet. Warships are built and moved like any ship; there is no war yet, so they
patrol (§17).

**Out of scope.** Fleets (42); war, battles and what the modules do in them (43, 44); repairs and the
repair bay (44); the troop transport, defences and their technologies (46); the rivals building
warships (43 and 48: until then they build none, and leave the Weapons field to last, as they leave
Translation, since it does nothing for them).

| Destroyer | 4 | 32 | 70, 20, 4 | 2 | Destroyer hulls |
| Cruiser | 6 | 55 | 120, 35, 8 | 3 | Cruiser hulls |

A warship is built at a shipyard like any ship, for its hull's cost and its modules'. Survey, outpost
and colony ships keep their upkeep of 1. Warships use lanes as other ships do and never jump blind.

Every place holds one module. Weapons have a damage type (Reborn's four), a damage a shot and a best

do in battle comes with increment 44; until then the screens show them as numbers.

### 21.3 Designs

- **A design** is a hull, a name and a module in each place. Once made it never changes, so every
  ship built to it stays as it was built; a design is **retired** to take it off the build lists.
- **The yard's design** for each hull a power can build is made from the best it knows: half its
  places (rounded up) weapons, alternating the two strongest kinds it knows (ion counted at half,
  as it barely scratches a hull), then a shield, then armour, and on a cruiser or capital ship a
  targeting computer once known and a second shield on a capital ship. Every power's yard follows
  the same rule (D-08). When research brings a better module or a new hull, the yard makes a new
  design as the turn ends (*Frigate Mark II*), retires its old one, and the Admiral says so.
- **The player's designs:** a hull, a name (up to 24 characters) and a module of any it knows in
  each place, through the order `design`; `retire-design` takes one off the lists. The rivals use
  the yard's.
- A ship's queue item names its design; an item queued before the design was retired is still
  built.

### 21.4 Refits

At a system where its power has a shipyard, a warship may be **refitted** to another design of its
hull: a queue item at that colony, costing the new design's modules less the old ones' (never less
than a tenth of the new modules' cost). Paid, the ship takes the new design as the turn ends. If it
has left the system, the refit is dropped unpaid and the report says so.

### 21.6 The Admiral

### 21.7 On the screen

- **Designs** (a sheet on a phone, a panel on a desktop with **B**), opened from the Empire's *Ships*
  tab and a shipyard's build list: every design that can be built, by hull, with its modules,

  and lists each place with the modules known for it, the numbers changing as they are chosen, and
  *Save design*; *Retire* on one's own designs.
- **The build list** offers each design that can be built (the yard's newest and the player's own),

- **A warship's panel** shows its design, hull, modules and numbers, and *Refit* at a shipyard when a
  newer design of its hull stands, with its cost.
- **The research screen** has the Weapons field as a seventh tab (phone) or column (desktop).

### 21.8 State

`PowerState.designs` (each: an id, a name, a hull, the modules, whether the yard's, the turn made,
and whether retired); `ShipState.design` (null for a civil ship); a ship's queue item names its
design; a refit is a queue item of its own. **State version 16**; a save from before gains the
yard's designs for the hulls it can build, and its ships no design.
