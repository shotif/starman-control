## 1. Where M4 starts

- **Five powers deal with each other** (M3): contact from a signal to talk, regard with its
  reasons, treaties (open lanes, shared research, borders, trade), exchanges, and the Hollow Wake's
  passage, bribes and protection. Protection is paid but does nothing yet: M3 promised that from
  M4 *the seller's ships leave the buyer's systems alone* (M3_NEIGHBOURS.md §23.4).
- **There are no warships.** Every ship is a survey, outpost or colony ship, ordered one at a time
  (M1). Ships pay 1 credit of upkeep a turn. Ships use their own power's lanes, and a partner's
  with open lanes or passage; only survey ships jump blind.
- **Nothing can be lost:** a colony in revolt stops but is never lost (D-35), and no game ends.
  The score (D-50) ranks the powers with no victory; D-14 (victory conditions) was left for M4.
- **The research tree** has six fields (Propulsion, Construction, Life Sciences, Society, Survey,
  Xenology) and 62 technologies. SCOPE §4.6 planned an *Energy and Weapons* field that was never
  built, since nothing needed it.
- **At turn 100 of the headless games** (M2_BALANCE.md) each power holds 6 to 16 colonies on
  average, 40 to 100 million people, and the rivals keep 40 to 230 credits.
- **The owner's try of M3** (100 turns or more, on a phone and a desktop) was asked for; what they
  find is folded in here when it comes.

## 2. Goals

- **Battles and wars** (D-58): powers build warships, gather them into fleets, go to war and make
  peace, fight battles that the player shapes with orders beforehand and can watch afterwards
  (D-07), and take each other's worlds.
- **Every battle explains itself:** who fought, what was lost, and why it went that way.
- **Designs that matter:** what a power researches and builds decides how its ships fight, and a
  counter can be found to an enemy's designs.
- **Wars with reasons and a price:** declared, remembered by everyone, paid for in upkeep and in
  weariness, and ended by peace or by surrender.
- **A game that ends:** a power can win by conquest or by the council's vote, and the player can
  lose (D-14).
- **The rivals fight by their character**, through the same orders as the player's (D-08), and the
  outlaw raids as M3 promised.
- **Phone and desktop alike** (D-04): every new screen on both, and the replay watchable on a phone.

## 3. Out of scope

Admirals and other named leaders (M5, SCOPE §4.14); espionage (M5); relics, the keys and the
victory they give (M5, D-13); carriers and fighters, stations that move, very large hulls (later,
SCOPE §4.7); tactical command of a battle (later, D-07); playing an alien power (later, D-08);
more species (M5).

## 4. Warships and their designs (D-59)

SCOPE §4.7: hulls from small to capital, a designer with slots for weapons, shields, armour, drives
and special modules, good designs offered automatically, and refits.

- **A. Hulls and modules, with the yard's designs and refits (recommended).** Five warship hulls,
  each with room for more modules: the **corvette** (2), **frigate** (3), **destroyer** (4),
  **cruiser** (6) and **capital ship** (8); and a sixth civil hull, the **troop transport** (§10).
  A module is a **weapon** (one of Reborn's four damage types: energy, kinetic, plasma, ion), a
  **shield** (Reborn's balanced, deflector or diffuser), **armour**, or a **special** (a targeting
  computer, a repair bay, a drive that adds a jump point), each in marks from research. A
  **design** is a hull with a module in each place, named. Each power's yard offers **its design
  for every hull** it can build, made from the best it knows and renewed when research brings
  better; the player may make and name their own, and the rivals use the yard's. A **refit** at a
  shipyard brings a ship to its hull's newest design for the difference in cost, in a turn. Each
  power's shipmakers name its warships and give them their look (§8); the rules are the same for
  every power.
- **B. Fixed classes, no designer.** Each hull comes in one design that research improves. The
  least to learn and to build, but nothing to counter an enemy with but numbers.
- **C. A full designer** in the manner of SCOPE's inspiration: many module kinds and sizes, power
  and space to balance, facings. Much to show on a phone, and a long increment.

## 5. The Weapons field (D-60)

- **A. A seventh field, Weapons (recommended)**, about 20 technologies over five tiers: weapon
  marks for each damage type, the three shields and their marks, armour, the destroyer, cruiser and
  capital hulls, defences (batteries, the planetary shield, barracks), troop transports and repair
  bays. It costs as the other fields do (with M2b's rising costs), so a power that arms learns less
  of the rest. Every power starts able to build corvettes and frigates with energy and kinetic
  guns, balanced shields and plate armour, so a war can be fought from the first turn.
- **B. Spread over the fields there are:** hulls in Construction, drives in Propulsion, shields in
  Energy… There is no Energy field, and a warlike power's choices would be hidden among the rest.

## 6. Fleets and their orders (D-61)

D-07 decided that *each task force's stance, target priority and retreat point are set before the
battle*. SCOPE §4.7 made fleets of task forces, each with a role (line, escort, screen, support,
transport).

- **A. Fleets with their own orders; fleets in one battle fight as task forces (recommended).** A
  **fleet** is ships of one power together in a system, named, ordered as one: it moves along lanes
  at the pace of its slowest ship, and it has a **stance** (engage, defend, evade), a **target
  priority** (warships, the largest, the smallest, transports) and a **retreat point** (when it has
  lost a quarter, half, three quarters, or never). Fleets are formed, merged and split at will;
  civil ships may sail with them (a colony ship under escort, a troop convoy). When several fleets
  of one power are in one battle, each fights as **a task force with its own orders**, so the
  player who wants a screen and a line of battle makes two fleets. One list of fleets is all a
  phone has to show.
- **B. Task forces inside each fleet,** each with a role and its own orders. As SCOPE drafted, but
  three to five sets of orders for every fleet before every battle, and a deeper sheet on a phone.
- **C. No fleets:** every ship ordered alone, as now. Simplest to build; at war, tens of ships to
  move one by one.

**What limits fleets**, under any option: **upkeep by hull size** (a corvette 1 credit a turn, a
capital ship 5), so fleets follow the economy (SCOPE §4.7's command limit is left out: upkeep does
its work with one number fewer); **damage that stays** after a battle; and **repairs** only near
home: quickly at a shipyard, slowly in a system with one's own colony, not at all elsewhere.

## 7. Battles (D-62)

**Where.** A battle is fought in a system where, after the turn's movement, armed ships or
defences of two powers hostile to each other are present: powers at war (§9), or a raider and the
power it raids (§11). A fleet that enters a system where a hostile power has armed ships or
defences **stops there** (it is intercepted), so lanes, chokepoints and the systems that guard them
matter (D-19).

- **A. Rounds at three ranges (recommended).** Up to eight rounds. Each task force closes to or
  holds the range its stance seeks (*engage*: close; *defend*: middle, where it stands; *evade*:
  long, leaving when it can). Each weapon has its best range (kinetic long, energy and ion middle,
  plasma close) and does less outside it. Each ship fires at targets its task force's priority
  chooses; hits are rolled from the game's seed, so the same game and the same orders give the same
  battle (D-06). Damage meets shields first, by Reborn's damage matrix (ion breaks shields and
  barely scratches a hull; deflectors shed kinetic slugs; diffusers spread energy), and shields
  recover a share each round; then armour, then the hull. A task force that has lost its retreat
  point leaves for its nearest own system. Defences fight as a task force of their own. Every
  battle is **reported**: the forces, the losses, the rounds in brief, and **why** ("their
  deflectors shed our railguns; plasma would burn through").
- **B. One comparison of strength.** Each side's strength, losses by the ratio, a line in the
  report. Quick, but little to plan and nothing to watch.

## 8. The replay (D-63)

D-07 decided *a short 3D replay to watch, skip or rewind*. How:

- **A. In 3D, in the system where it was fought (recommended).** The system's star and worlds as the
  system view draws them (M1, D-25), and the ships drawn procedurally in each power's look and
  colour, from Reborn's ship kit (D-09, D-10): the Authority's balanced Halden and armoured Ares
  hulls, the Cooperative's frugal, fast and cheap makers', the Wake's salvaged hulls rebuilt, and a
  look of their own for the Duskfolk's and the Drifters' ships (fiction). Shots, shields flaring
  and ships breaking up, round by round, in about 30 seconds; play, pause, a round at a time,
  rewind, faster, and *skip*; the report beside it. On a phone, full screen with the controls in
  thumb reach. No pictures to buy.
- **B. A 2D schematic:** the task forces in lines at their ranges, shots as lines between them.
  Lighter and clearer on a small phone, less to see.
- **C. The report only in M4,** the replay later.

## 9. War and peace (D-64)

D-53 left non-aggression, alliance and war for M4.

- **A. Declared war, peace, pacts and capitulation (recommended).**
  - **War is declared** (an order). It ends every treaty between the two at once and is remembered:
    by the other (*declared war on us*), and less by every power in contact with the declarer, more
    by the lawful.
  - **At war** fleets fight where they meet; warships may use the enemy's open lanes (*forced
    passage*), stopped by its fleets and defences; colonies may be invaded and bombarded (§10).
  - **Peace** is offered and answered as a treaty: each keeps what it holds, an exchange may go
    with it (M3's), and neither may declare war on the other for 10 turns.
  - **Non-aggression** (a treaty): to declare war it must first be broken (M3's breach: −20, heard
    by all). **Alliance** (a treaty): open lanes, each sees the other's ships, and each is asked
    to join a war declared on the other (the rivals join when warm).
  - **Capitulation:** a power at war may surrender to another. It keeps what it holds but is
    **subdued**: it pays a fifth of its taxes as tribute, cannot declare war, and is at war with
    whoever its victor fights. A subdued power counts as beaten for conquest (§13). The rivals
    surrender when they have lost their capital or most of their colonies.
  - **War weariness:** each turn at war, and each ship and colony lost, costs approval at every
    colony, a little at first and more as the war goes on, by each power's character (the
    Cooperative tires fastest). Peace lets it fade.
- **B. War and peace only.** No pacts, no alliances, no surrender: conquest then means taking every
  colony of every rival, across 33 light-years.

## 10. Defences and invasion (D-65)

SCOPE §4.8: planetary shields, batteries and garrisons built as projects; troop transports landing
armies; resolved as radio traffic from the commanders to Control; bombardment and blockade.

- **A. All of SCOPE §4.8 (recommended).**
  - **Defences** are projects: the **defence battery** (guns that fight in any battle at its
    system, with its power's best weapon), the **planetary shield** (stops bombardment and shields
    the batteries) and **barracks** (a larger garrison). Each is a building in the colony view.
  - **Garrison:** every colony has troops from its people, and more with barracks; an outpost has
    none.
  - **Troop transports** (a civil hull) carry troops raised at the colony that builds them.
  - **Invasion:** transports in a system where the enemy has no armed ships or defences left land
    their troops on a colony as the turn ends; troops and garrison fight, rolled from the seed. If
    the invaders win, the colony is theirs, with its people (unhappy for a while) and buildings; a
    capital taken moves its power's capital to its largest colony. An outpost falls to any troops.
    Each landing is told as **radio traffic**, the commanders' words to Control.
  - **Bombardment** (an order): a fleet holding a system's orbit kills people and wrecks buildings
    each turn, unless a planetary shield stands; every power that hears of it remembers.
  - **Blockade:** a colony whose orbit a hostile power holds is cut off from its power's food and
    trade, and makes less.
- **B. Defences and invasion only;** no bombardment or blockade.

## 11. Raids and protection (D-66)

M3 sold protection from the Wake's ships before they could do anything (§23.4).

- **A. The outlaw raids without war (recommended).** A power that is not lawful (the Hollow Wake)
  may order a fleet to **raid** a system of a power it is not at war with: it fights that power's
  armed ships and defences there, and if it holds the orbit it takes **plunder** each turn
  (credits from a colony, a resource from an outpost). A raid is remembered by the raided and by
  every lawful power that hears of it, but it is not war: no invasion, no bombardment. **Protection
  is kept:** a power that sells protection may not raid its buyers, and the Wake as a rival raids
  those near it who do not pay.
- **B. Raids only in war.** The Wake is like any other power, and protection buys nothing but
  regard.

## 12. The council (D-67)

SCOPE §4.9: the council forms once enough powers know each other; its members vote on resolutions
and on who leads it; elected leader by a large majority is a victory (D-14). Its name and rules are
to be designed.

- **A. Elections and two resolutions (recommended).** **The Assembly** (a working name, fiction)
  forms the first time three powers can each talk with the other two; any power that can talk with
  a member joins. Each member has **a vote for every million of its people**, as SCOPE's
  inspiration weighed its senate by population. Every 20 turns (five years) it **elects a leader**:
  the two members with the most votes stand, and every member votes for one or abstains (the
  rivals by their regard). **A candidate with two thirds of all the members' votes is elected and
  wins the game.** Between elections a member may propose one of two **resolutions**: *sanctions*
  on a member at war with another (every member ends its trade with it), or *a call for peace* to
  two members at war (sanctions on whichever refuses peace within 5 turns). A member's vote follows
  its regard. A subdued power votes as its victor does.
- **B. Elections only:** the victory path, with nothing for the Assembly to do between elections.
- **C. More resolutions:** A, and lane law, embargoes, expelling a member, joint fleets. Later, if
  the Assembly is too quiet.

## 13. Victory and defeat (D-14)

D-14 recommended *conquest, a council vote, the artefact hunt, and a score at a turn limit*. In
M4:

- **A. Conquest, the Assembly's vote, and a score at a turn limit (recommended).**
  - **Conquest:** every other power in the game has lost its last colony or has capitulated to
    you.
  - **The Assembly's choice:** elected its leader by two thirds of the votes (§12).
  - **At a turn limit** (a new-game setting: 150, 200 or 250 turns, or none; 250 by default, the
    long end of D-16's game), the highest score (D-50) wins.
  - **The keys** come with M5.
  - **Defeat:** your last colony lost, your capitulation, or another power's victory. A lost power
    leaves the game; its outposts and ships go with it, and its lanes go dark.
  - **The end:** a screen of victory or defeat with the story of the game in numbers; after a
    victory the game can be played on.
- **B. Conquest and the vote, with no turn limit.** A game goes on until someone wins it.

## 14. Rivals at war (D-68)

- **A. By character (recommended)**, through the same orders as the player's, and always for a
  reason the player can read (a hostile regard, a claim crossed, an outlaw's raids, an ally's call):
  - **The Transit Authority** (law) keeps a fleet at home and declares war only on a power it holds
    hostile or one that raids it; it makes peace once its losses mount.
  - **The Frontier Co-op** (peace) never declares war, defends, joins alliances, and is the first to
    make peace.
  - **The Hollow Wake** (outlaw) raids those near it who do not buy protection, declares war on a
    weaker neighbour it regards coldly, and sells peace for credits.
  - **The Dusk Concord** (slow to forget) declares war on a power that keeps settling in its claim,
    when it is the stronger, and is slow to make peace.
  - **The Drift Moot** (brokers) never declares war, buys protection, and pays for peace.
  - Every rival arms when a neighbour arms (the Watch sees it), defends its colonies, invades where
    it is stronger, and surrenders when it has lost its capital or most of its colonies.
- **B. Strength alone:** every rival declares war on whoever is weaker. More wars, fewer reasons.
- **C. No rival starts a war:** they defend, and the Wake still raids. A gentle game; conquest then
  is only the player's.

The headless games show how often wars come and who wins (§18).

## 15. Words from the other powers (D-17)

D-17 asked whether text should be written live by a language model while playing (for example,
alien envoys speaking): declarations of war, offers of peace and radio traffic make the question
real.

- **A. Every line written into the game (recommended).** As every line is now, in each power's own
  voice, with variants chosen by the seed. A public static site cannot hold an API key (D-09), and
  every line can be read and checked before it ships.
- **B. Lines written at build time** with Gemini, reviewed and committed like the pictures: more
  variety for a few cents, and the same checks.
- **C. The player's own key** in their browser, for live words from the envoys: possible on a
  static site, but the words cannot be checked, need the network, and are out of scope for 1.0
  (SCOPE §5).

## 16. On the screen

- **Fleets on the map:** one mark for a fleet, with its number of ships and its power's colour and
  shape; a hostile fleet in sight marked as such. Selecting a fleet shows its card: its ships,
  strength, damage and orders, with *Move*, *Stance*, *Target*, *Retreat*, *Split*, *Merge*,
  *Raid*, *Invade* and *Bombard* where they apply. On a phone the Empire's *Ships* tab becomes
  *Fleets*; on a desktop a fleets panel with **F**.
- **The designer:** from a shipyard's queue and the Empire. On a phone, a sheet for each design (its
  hull, then each place and its module as a list, with what the design does in numbers); on a
  desktop, the hull's places beside the modules, with two designs compared.
- **Battles in the report,** each with *Watch*, which opens the replay (§8); *what needs you*: a
  fleet that retreated, a colony under blockade or siege, an offer of peace, an election.
- **War and peace on the diplomacy screen:** *Declare war*, with what it will cost said first; peace,
  non-aggression, alliance and capitulation among the treaties.
- **The Assembly:** a screen of its own with its members, their votes, the next election and the
  resolutions; from the menu and the Powers tab on a phone, **A** on a desktop.
- **The Admiral:** an eighth advisor for each power, the voice of its fleets in the report, with a
  face (3 portraits for the human powers).
- **The end:** a screen of victory or defeat.

## 21. Increment 41 in detail: warships (written before building it)

**Goals.** D-59 and D-60 as decided (A): five warship hulls, modules for each place, designs made
from them, the yard's design for every hull a power can build, renewed as research brings better,
the player's own designs, refits at a shipyard, and the Weapons field's technologies for hulls,
weapons, shields, armour and specials. Upkeep by hull size (D-61). The Admiral, an eighth advisor,
speaks for the fleet. Warships are built and moved like any ship; there is no war yet, so they

### 23.1 War

- **Order `declare-war`:** on a power it is in contact with. Refused while a truce holds (*we made
  peace on Q2 2181: no war before Q4 2183*), while a non-aggression pact or an alliance holds
  (*break it first*), and on a power it is already at war with.
- **It takes effect at once.** Every other treaty between the two ends, and every offer between
  them is dropped. The other remembers it (**−30**, *Declared war on us*); every other power in
  contact with the declarer hears of it (**−5**, or **−10** for a lawful power, *Declared war on
  the Frontier Co-op*). While the war lasts the other counts **−20** in its regard (*At war with
  us*).
- **Allies:** every power with an alliance with the one attacked is at war with the declarer too,
  at once (§23.3). The diplomacy screen says so before war is declared.
- A war is between two powers; each of an alliance's wars is its own.

### 23.2 Peace

- **Offered** (order `offer`, kind `peace`) to a power we are at war with; answered as any offer
  (a rival as the turn ends, the player in the next turn). Accepted: the war ends, each keeps what
  it holds, and a **truce** of 10 turns follows, in which neither may declare war on the other.
- **A rival makes peace** when the war has lasted 5 turns or more (increment 48 weighs its losses
  and gains), and **offers peace** every 5 turns once the war has lasted 10.

## 24. Increment 44 in detail: battles (written before building it)

**Goals.** D-62 as decided (A): a **battle** wherever, after the turn's movement, armed ships of two
powers at war meet, or armed ships meet the ships of a power at war with theirs. It is fought in
rounds at three ranges by the rules below, from the game's seed, so the same game and the same
orders give the same battle (D-06). Each fleet fights as a task force by its own stance, target
and retreat point (D-07, D-61). Damage stays after the battle and is **repaired** near home. Every
battle is **reported**, with why it went as it did, and kept for the replay (45). The rivals at war
build warships to defend themselves.

**Out of scope.** The replay (45); defences and invasion (46); raids (47); the rivals attacking
(48).

### 24.1 Where and who

- After movement (and before arrivals), every system where a power has an **armed** ship (a warship
  with a weapon) and a power at war with it has any ship is a battle. Every power there at war with
  another there takes part.
- **Task forces:** each fleet of a power there is a task force with its fleet's orders; a power's
  ships sailing alone there form one task force of their own (*evade*, *warships*, *never*).
- A ship fires only at ships of powers at war with its own.

### 24.2 Rounds and ranges

- Up to **8 rounds**. Every task force starts at **long** range and moves a step towards its
  stance's range at the start of each round, before firing: *engage* close, *defend* middle, *evade*
  long. Between two task forces the
  range is the nearer of their two (the bolder sets it).
- An *evade* task force leaves after the **second** round, having taken the long-range fire of the
  first two.
- Each weapon fires once a round at its best range for its full damage, **60%** a step away and
  **30%** two steps away (kinetic best at long, energy and ion at middle, plasma at close).

### 24.3 Hits and targets

- **To hit:** 70 in 100, **+15** with a targeting computer, **−15** against a task force that
  evades; against a corvette −10, a frigate −5, a cruiser +5, a capital ship +10.
- **Targets**, by the firing task force's priority: *warships* (armed ships, then the rest), *the
  largest* (most structure left), *the smallest*, *transports* (ships with no weapon first); among
  the first group, one chosen at random from the seed.

### 24.4 Damage

- Worked in tenths. A hit meets the target's **shields** first, by Reborn's damage matrix (copied
  as percentages): energy 130 against deflectors, 70 against diffusers, 100 balanced, 90 on a hull;
  kinetic 70, 100, 100, 120; plasma 90, 130, 100, 100; ion 160, 160, 150, 25. A ship's shields are
  its shield modules' capacity together, of the kind of its strongest. What they do not stop reaches
  the **structure**.
- Shields recover **a fifth** of their capacity after each round. Structure lost stays (the ship's
  `damage`). A ship with no structure left is **destroyed**.

### 24.5 Retreat and the end

- A task force whose ships have lost its **retreat point** of their structure at the start (a
  quarter, half, three quarters; never at *never*) leaves after that round.
- The battle ends after 8 rounds, or when no side has an armed ship still fighting, or no side has
  an enemy still in the battle.
- Ships that left go home: their fleet makes for the nearest other system with a colony of its own,
  by lanes (a lone ship likewise); with none to go to, they stay.
- **Who won:** the side with armed ships still in the system; if both have, neither, and they fight
  again next turn.

### 24.6 Repairs

As the turn ends, a damaged warship is repaired: **half** its structure a turn at a system where its
power has a shipyard, **a tenth** at one where it has a colony, and nothing elsewhere; a **repair
bay** in its fleet repairs every ship of the fleet a tenth a turn anywhere. A new Weapons
technology, **Field repairs** (tier 2, 60, inspired by a battle fought), opens the repair bay (a
special module: 10 industry, 4 metals, 2 volatiles).

### 24.7 Weariness and memory

Each warship lost adds **2** to its power's war weariness, each other ship **1**.

### 24.8 The report and the battle kept

- **Each power** in a battle reads *a battle at Ross 128*: the forces on each side, the ships lost
  and damaged, who held the system, and up to three lines of **why**, chosen from what happened:
  their shields against our weapons and ours against theirs (*their deflectors shed our railguns: a
  third of our fire was lost; plasma would burn through*), the ranges the stances made (*our evade
  kept us at long range, where their plasma was weak*), the weight of fire on each side, a retreat.
- **The battle is kept** for the turn (`GameState.battles`): the ships, their task forces, and each
  round's moves and shots, for the replay; the next turn's battles replace it.

### 24.9 The rivals at war

A rival at war (only if attacked, until 48) builds warships of its yard's newest design at each
shipyard while its treasury allows, and keeps them at its capital on *defend*. It researches the
Weapons field once it holds warships, as increment 41 set out.

`ShipState.damage` (structure lost, in tenths); `GameState.battles` (the last turn's); the battle
report event. **State version 19**; a save from before has no damage and no battles.

## 25. Increment 45 in detail: the replay (written before building it)

**Goals.** D-63 as decided (A): any battle of the turn can be **watched** from the report, in 3D,

from Reborn's ship kit; the task forces closing to their ranges round by round; shots by the kind
of their weapon; shields flaring; ships breaking up; task forces leaving. It plays in about 30
seconds, and can be paused, stepped a round at a time, rewound, played faster and skipped to the
end. The report's account of the battle stays beside it. It is first-class on a phone (full
screen, the controls in thumb reach) and on a desktop (the account beside the scene, keys for the
controls). Its code loads only when a battle is watched.

**Out of scope.** Sound (none in M4); battles of earlier turns (the turn's battles only, as kept in
§24.8); choosing a camera per ship; pictures (none to buy).

### 25.1 What is shown

- **The place:** the map's Milky Way behind, as the system view has it, and the system's chief star
  as a disc of its colour far off, so a battle at a red dwarf looks red-lit. The battle is fought in
  open space; worlds are not drawn (fiction: battles happen where fleets meet, not by a world).
- **The sides:** each power in the battle takes a bearing round the centre (the player's power, if
  it fought, on the near side, facing away from the camera); each of its task forces a row along
  that bearing, its ships in a line abreast, larger hulls behind. A task force's **distance** from
  the centre shows its range: long, middle or close. Between rounds it moves to its new range.
- **The ships:** one model for each power, from Reborn's kit (D-10), with its colour as the accent
  and a ring of its colour under each ship, as the map marks it:
  - the Transit Authority: Reborn's Kite courier, its accent in the Authority's green;
  - the Frontier Co-op: Reborn's Hauler, frugal and long;
  - the Hollow Wake: Reborn's raider, salvaged gunmetal and rust;
  - the Dusk Concord (fiction): a dark crescent hull with amber slits, for a people of the dusk;
  - the Drift Moot (fiction): clustered pods like their floaters, violet-lit.
  Sizes follow the hull: a corvette smallest, a capital ship about three times its length; civil
  ships small.
- **Shots,** each from the log in its round, coloured by its weapon's damage type: energy a
  blue-white bolt, kinetic a fast orange tracer, plasma a slow green bloom, ion a violet crackle. A

- **A ship destroyed** flashes, tumbles and breaks into debris that drifts out and fades. **A task

- **Labels:** each task force's name over it in its power's colour, placed by the labels' layout
  (as on the map); a ship chosen (a tap or a click) shows its name, design and structure left.

### 25.2 The timeline

A pure model of the replay (`src/render/replayModel.ts`), from the battle's log alone, gives where

- An opening of **1.5 s** (the forces at long range), then **3.5 s a round**: the first **0.8 s**

  order; a closing of **1.5 s**. Eight rounds take 31 s.
- A shot flies for a time by its kind (kinetic 0.25 s, energy 0.4 s, plasma 0.7 s, ion 0.15 s) and
  takes its shields and structure when it lands. Shields recover a fifth of their capacity at the
  end of a round, as in the battle (§24.4).
- A ship is destroyed when the shot that ends it lands. A task force leaves in the last 0.6 s of its
  last round.
- The model gives, for a time: each ship's place, heading, structure and shields left, and whether
  it is fighting, breaking up, leaving or gone; the shots in flight and their impacts; each side's
  ships and structure left; and the round.

**Out of scope.** Raids (47); the rivals invading and bombarding (48: until then they defend);

### 26.1 Defences

Three projects for a colony (not an outpost), opened by three new Weapons technologies (§26.8):

| Project | Cost (industry, metals, volatiles) | Upkeep | Does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Defence battery** | 60, 15, 0 | 2 | Fights in any battle at its system: three of its power's best weapon, a targeting computer, structure 60. Beaten, it is silenced for that battle and fires again the next turn. |
| **Planetary shield** | 120, 20, 10 | 3 | Stops bombardment; gives the battery 30 of balanced shields. |
| **Barracks** | 40, 10, 0 | 1 | Ten more troops in the garrison. |

### 26.2 The garrison

Every colony has troops from its people: **2, and 1 for each million people**, 5 more at the
capital, 10 more with barracks. An outpost has none. A colony's panel says its garrison; a rival's
colony in sight says its garrison and defences too (the Watch sees them).

### 26.3 Troop transports

A sixth civil hull, the **troop transport** (cost 30 industry, 5 metals, 5 fuel; upkeep 1;
structure 10), opened by *Ground forces*. It carries **5 troops**, raised from the people of the
colony that builds it: a tenth of a million, so a colony of less than a million cannot build one.
Like a warship, a transport built joins the fleet waiting at its yard or forms one; as a civil ship
it fires at no one, and *transports first* targets it.

### 26.4 Invasion

- **The order:** *Invade* a colony or outpost, on a fleet with transports in a system where it is,
  of a power at war with the fleet's. It stands until it is carried out, or the fleet moves or is
  given another.
- **As the turn ends,** after the battles: if the target's power has no armed ship and no battery
  still fighting in the system, every transport of the fleet lands its troops. Troops and garrison
  fight it out, one against one, from the seed: the garrison wins each exchange **55 in 100**,
  until one side is gone. The transports are spent either way.
- **Taken:** the colony is the invader's, with its people and its buildings (a great work too); its
  queue, stored industry, governor and locks are cleared; its people are **unhappy for 10 turns**
  (approval −20). A capital taken moves its power's capital to its largest colony left. An outpost
  falls to any troops that land. The loser's war weariness rises by 5, and it remembers the
  invasion (−10).
- **Radio traffic:** both powers read of it in the report, with their commanders' words to Control
  (the invader's officer as the troops land, fight and win or fall back; the garrison's as it sees
  them come, holds and falls or stands), in each power's voice, with the numbers of the fight.

### 26.5 Bombardment

- **The order:** *Bombard* a colony, on a fleet with an armed ship in its system, of a power at war
  with the fleet's; *Stop* ends it.
- **Each turn's end,** while the fleet holds the orbit (as §26.4) and there is no planetary shield:
  the colony loses **a twentieth of its people** (at least a tenth of a million), and one turn in
  three its newest building is **wrecked** (never a great work). A planetary shield stops it all.
- Both powers read of it every turn. It is remembered: −15 by the bombed, −5 by every other power
  in contact with the bombarder, −10 by the lawful.

### 26.6 Blockade

A colony is **blockaded** while armed ships of a power at war with its own are in its system and
none of its own power's armed ships or batteries is: then it is **cut off from its supply network**
(it lives on its own food, without the store) and its **credits are halved**. Its panel says so, and
the report tells its power when a blockade begins.

### 26.7 Batteries in battle

- A battle is fought (§24.1) wherever a power has a battery and a power at war with it has any ship;
  and a battery stops ships at war with its power as a warship does (interception, §23.4).
- A colony's battery fights as **a task force of its own** (*its colony's defences*), never moving or
  leaving: the range between it and an enemy is the enemy's. It fires with three of its power's
  best weapon (the strongest weapon module it can build), a targeting computer, structure 60, and
  the planetary shield's 30 if one stands. It is hit as a capital ship is (+10).
- It counts as armed for holding the system. Silenced, it is in the log among those lost, and fires
  again the next turn: a battery is never destroyed.
- In the replay, a battery is a fortress platform at its side, its power's colour beneath.

## 27. Increment 47 in detail: raids (written before building it)

**Goals.** D-66 as decided (A): the outlaw **raids** without war. A power that is not lawful (the
Hollow Wake) may send a fleet to raid a colony or outpost of a power it is not at war with: it
fights that power's armed ships and batteries there, and while it holds the orbit it takes
**plunder** each turn. A raid is remembered, but it is not war: no invasion, no bombardment.
**Protection is kept** (M3_NEIGHBOURS.md §23.4): a power that sells protection never raids its
buyers. As a rival, the Wake raids those near it who do not pay.

**Out of scope.** The other rivals going to war (48); the Assembly's sanctions (49).

### 27.1 The raid

- **The order:** *Raid* a colony or outpost, a fleet's mission (as §26.4), on a fleet with an armed
  ship in the target's system, of a power that is not lawful. Refused against a power at war with
  it (then invade or bombard), one that buys protection from it, one it has an alliance or a
  non-aggression pact with (break it first), and one it has a truce with.
- **Hostile where it raids:** the raiding power and the raided are hostile in that system as if at
  war, for as long as the raid lasts: their ships there fight (§24), and the raided power's
  batteries there fire on the raiders. Elsewhere they are at peace: neither stops the other's
  ships, and neither may land troops or bombard.
- **Plunder:** as the turn ends, after the battles, while the raided power does not hold the orbit
  (§26.4): from a colony, **its credits of the turn** (taxes and markets) are taken from its
  power's treasury and go to the raider's; from an outpost, **its yields of the turn** (metals,
  volatiles, fuel) go from its power's stockpile to the raider's. Both read of it.
- **Remembered:** −10 by the raided, renewed while the raid goes on rather than piled up; −5 by every
  lawful power in contact with the raider that hears of it.
- **The raid ends** when its fleet moves or is given another mission, or when it would now be
  refused (protection bought, war declared, a pact made).

### 27.2 Protection kept

Protection bought from the Wake (5 credits a turn, M3) now does what M3 promised: the Wake cannot
raid its buyers. The treaty's words say so.

### 27.3 The Wake as a rival

- It keeps a **raiding squadron** of two warships of its yard's newest design, built at its yards
  as the treasury allows (and more at war, as §24.9).
- Every turn, an idle armed fleet of its (no mission, not moving, at least two warships) picks the
  nearest colony or outpost, by lanes and within four jumps, of a power in contact that it may raid
  (§27.1), the least defended first (no armed ship or battery there), and makes for it; there, it
  raids. When the raid ends it goes home, as other fleets do.

### 27.4 On the screen

- **A fleet's panel** (a power that is not lawful): *Raid* for each colony and outpost in its
  system that it may raid, saying what it would take each turn; the mission in the fleet's line.
- **The report:** the plunder taken or lost (*the Hollow Wake's 1st Pack raided Proxima Centauri b
  and took 12 credits*), and the battles a raid brings, with *Watch*.
- **The diplomacy screen:** protection's words: *the Wake's ships will not raid you*.

### 27.5 State

`Mission.kind` gains `raid`; memories `raided` and `raided-heard`; the report event `raided`. No
field changes shape, so the state's version stays 21.

### Increment 47: raids (22 October 2026)

- **The raid as §27.1:** a fleet's mission (`raid`) for a power that is not lawful, refused as
  designed (`raidRefusal`, `mayRaid` in `src/sim/invasion.ts`). Raider and raided are hostile in that
  system only (`hostileAt` in `src/sim/war.ts`), so battles and batteries there treat them as at war;
  plunder at step 1c of the turn (`src/sim/raids.ts`). One change found while testing: in the
  headless games no raid ever happened, as the Wake's ships could sail only its own lanes. So, as
  forced passage at war, **an outlaw's armed fleet may sail the lanes of every power it may raid**
  (`fleetLanes`); a power that buys its protection or has a pact with it keeps its lanes closed.
- **The report:** the plunder taken or lost, and a battle's line now names those that fought,
  whether at war or not.
- **The Wake as §27.3,** with two changes found in the headless games: it builds its squadron only
  **from turn 30 and with four colonies** (built at once, it cost the Wake two or three colonies by
  turn 100), and picks targets by one search of the lanes, not a route to each.
- **Numbers:** at turn 100 every power within M3's targets (the Authority 37.5 technologies, the
  others 19.8 to 23.5); in the eight games 76 turns of plunder and 4 battles. `npm run balance`
  now counts raids, battles, wars and colonies taken.
- **Tests:** `raids.test.ts` (7 tests); `raids.spec.ts` on the phone, tablet and desktop.
