## 1. Where M5 starts

- **Five powers** (the Transit Authority, the Frontier Co-op, the Hollow Wake, the Dusk Concord and
  the Drift Moot) on 356 real systems out to 33 light-years. They meet, deal, go to war, sit in the
  Assembly, and one of them wins: by conquest, by the Assembly's choice, or by score at the turn
  limit (M4).
- **The unknown is thin.** Twenty anomalies, investigated by survey ships for research or
  resources; three of them (*ordered dust*, *charts older than any*, *a second tone*) are the first
  threads of a mystery that is never followed up. Twelve events, each with two choices. Xenology,
  the field for the unknown, holds two technologies (Lapse studies and Translation).
- **No one has a name.** The advisors are the only people in the game; there are no leaders.
- **What others hold is seen only by survey** and by the Watch's rumours; there are no agents.
- **The headless games** (M2_BALANCE.md) are all won on score at turn 250, by three powers: the
  Co-op five times in eight, the Concord twice, the Wake once. The research leader, the Authority,
  never wins: D-69 left it a road through the keys, which this milestone builds.
- **Starman Reborn** (the canon, D-10) has no aliens, precursors or relics. It has threads to pull:
  derelicts whose charts are *older than any in your computer*, one whose bridge someone *switched
  off, carefully, and left*, carrier tones *as there has been for a very long time*, and **Pyre**,
  its one invented star, a red supergiant 33 light-years toward Phoenix that dies in Reborn and
  leaves a black hole, one lane from the real white dwarf GJ 915. Betelgeuse explodes in Reborn and
  Antares goes dark; Control is set about 150 years later (D-20).

## 2. Goals

1. **The unknown pulls you outward** (VISION, pillar 5): places worth going to, mostly at the real
   dead stars of the neighbourhood, with something to find that changes what you can do.
2. **A fourth road to victory, the keys,** open to every power, and the natural road for a power
   that studies more than it settles (the Authority's, D-69).
3. **A mystery told once, and well,** in chapters, quiet and uneasy rather than epic (VISION's
   tone); which relics lie where changes every game.
4. **People with names:** leaders, hired and lost, who grow with what they do.
5. **Knowing more about others,** for a price: light espionage.
6. **A living sky:** more events, and one set piece that changes the neighbourhood in mid-game.
7. **More of the others:** the next species, each mechanically different, and still none of them
   assuming the player is human (D-08).

## 3. Out of scope

- Sabotage, theft of technology and inciting unrest (SCOPE §4.10, *later*).
- Aliens as the player's power (D-08: later). Every new species is designed with enough to be
  played, as M3's were.
- The under-ice thinkers and the chorus (§12): after M5.
- Wider map sizes, the guided opening's update and balance from AI-against-AI games (M6).
- Live text from a language model while playing (D-17).

## 4. The Absent and their mystery (D-13)

Everything in this section is **fiction**, and the game labels it so.

**The story, as the game tells it in chapters.** Long before anyone here looked up, a people lived
among the nearby stars and then left. They left things behind, mostly at the dead stars: the white
dwarfs, the embers of stars that burned out (that they are white dwarfs is real; nineteen are on the
map, from Sirius B to GJ 3112). The powers call them **the Absent**, for the one thing everyone
agrees on. What they left is tidy and switched off, as if someone meant to come back.

The threads already in the game are theirs. The ordered dust is their work. The charts older than
any are copies of their charts, and the stars on them stand where they stood long ago: how far each
has moved since is exactly what astronomers measure today (the motions are real; the charts are
fiction), which dates them. And under the old beacons' carrier tones there is a second, slower tone,
which no one built. Since Pyre died, it has come from Pyre's direction.

The keys are **pieces of one thing**, an instrument for answering that tone. The story never says
what answers, or who listens. The last chapter is the power's own choice, made by building the
**Answer** (§6), and the end screen says only what the power hears.

**Told in chapters.** The mystery is twelve short revelations, the same in every game, each a few
lines in Reborn's plain voice. A power reads the next as it completes chapters of excavation
(§5), whichever sites they were at, so the story comes in order however the relics fall. Each
species reads them in its own words where its view differs (the drifters hear the tone as weather;
the twilight folk as a clock). They are kept in a journal, *The Absent*, in the codex.

**Options:**

- **A. The Absent (recommended):** a people who left, tied to Reborn's derelicts, its carrier tones
  and Pyre, with the white dwarfs as their places and the keys as an instrument for answering the
  second tone. What answers is never said.
- **B. The heirs' makers:** the precursors were the makers of the machine heirs (§12), whose ruins
  are relics and whose archive is the keys. Simpler to explain; the mystery becomes a family
  history.
- **C. No single story:** relics and keys with no account behind them. Least writing; least pull.

A folds in B's best part: the machine heirs (§12) are the Absent's machines, left running, and know
more than they say.

## 5. Relic sites (D-70)

- **Where.** Twelve sites in each game, placed by the seed: many at the white dwarfs, the rest at
  brown dwarfs (*a star that never caught light*) and other systems, each at least 8 light-years from
  every capital but within reach of one. Never at a capital. Each site is on a body of its system, or
  in its belt, or in deep space.
- **Found by survey.** A survey of the system finds the site and names its kind; nothing is known of
  what lies deeper. Six kinds, each with its own picture and three chapters: *a vault*, *a field of
  ordered dust*, *an array*, *a hollow moon*, *a hall of charts* and *a quiet beacon*.
- **Excavated in chapters, paid in research.** A power holding a colony or an outpost in the system
  opens an excavation there. Its research is shared between its current technology and its
  excavations, at a share it sets (none, a quarter or a half; a quarter by default); each chapter
  needs research points (rising: 150, 300, 500). The Authority, which researches most, digs
  fastest: its road (D-69). One power excavates a site at a time; when a site's holding changes
  hands, so does its excavation.
- **Each chapter ends in a choice,** as an event does: two choices, with what each costs and gives
  said before choosing. The rewards are research for a technology, resources, a technology of
  Xenology, regard, or nothing but a warning; a few choices are risky (to *open it* gives more than
  to *study it from outside*, and may wake its guardians, §7). The third chapter of five sites ends
  in a **key** (§6).
- **The mystery's next revelation** comes with every chapter a power completes (§4).
- **Xenology grows** from two technologies to seven: *Relic studies* (opens excavations), *Old
  charts* (the sites' kinds seen from afar), *Field archaeology* (each chapter needs a fifth less),
  *The second tone* (where the keys lie is known), *The instrument* (opens the Answer).

**Options:** **A. excavated in chapters, paid in research (recommended)** · B. investigated by a
survey ship, as an anomaly is, with no chapters · C. a dozen fixed sites, the same every game.

## 6. The keys and the Answer (D-71)

- **Five keys** in each game, at the end of five sites' third chapters. Every key site is guarded
  (§7).
- **A key stays where it was found,** in the holding that excavated it. A holding taken by invasion
  takes its key with it to the victor; a power that leaves the game leaves its keys at their sites,
  to be found again by one chapter more. Keys are not traded.
- **Holding keys is feared:** every other power regards a power 5 less for each key it holds
  (*Holds a key of the Absent*).
- **The Answer.** A power that holds all five keys and has learnt *The instrument* begins the Answer:
  10 turns, while it keeps the keys and its capital (§21.3). Every power in contact hears when it
  begins, and each gains a reason for war, whatever its character (*you would answer for us all*);
  the peaceful ones think much the less of it instead. If the capital is taken, or a key is lost,
  it stops, and must be begun again.
- **When it is done, that power wins** (a fourth kind of victory, *by the keys*, D-14). The end
  screen tells what it hears, in a few lines, and no more.

**Options:** **A. all five keys, then the Answer (recommended)** · B. the most keys at the turn
limit adds to the score, with no victory of their own · C. three keys, for shorter games.

## 7. Guardians (D-72)

- **Every key site, and about a third of the others,** is watched by guardians: old machines of the
  Absent that do not talk, do not move and do not leave (fiction). A guarded site cannot be
  excavated until its guardians are beaten.
- **They fight as a colony's battery does** (M4_FLEETS.md §26.7): a force that never moves, owned by
  no power, fought by any armed fleet sent to the site with the order *clear the guardians*. Their
  strength rises with the site's depth; a fleet that loses goes home; guardians beaten are gone for
  good; guardians not beaten mend in a few turns.
- **The battle is watched as any other,** in 3D, with the guardians' own models, drawn in code.
- **Waking them:** a risky choice in an unguarded site's chapter may wake guardians there.

**Options:** **A. guardians as fixed defences at the sites (recommended)** · B. none in M5.

**Deferred (22 October 2026, after increment 53).** Every battle, its replay and its report assume
each side is a power; guardians that belong to none need a side of their own throughout, a large
change. And in the headless games no rival yet gathers more than two keys: guardians would make the
keys harder still. So M5 goes on without them (B, for now); they come back with M6's balance, when
the rivals can carry the long campaign the keys need.

## 8. Leaders (D-73)

- **Named people, fiction,** in five posts: an **admiral** commands a fleet, a **governor** a
  colony, a **scientist** the power's research, an **envoy** its dealings and its seat in the
  Assembly, an **agent** its espionage (§9). A post makes a difference a player can read: an admiral
  adds to a fleet's attack, a governor to a colony's approval, and so on.
- **Hired from a pool** that offers a few candidates every 10 turns, each with a hiring fee and a
  wage in credits; a power has as many leaders as it can pay.
- **Traits that grow:** each has one or two of about fifteen traits; experience in the post raises
  one level of a trait (to 3). A leader may also bring a flaw.
- **Loyalty that can fail:** a leader whose power is in debt, or whose colony is unhappy, may leave,
  and a rival may lure one away (the *defectors* event grows into this). An admiral can die with
  the fleet.
- **Names and faces** by species and power: the human powers' names in their own styles (the
  Authority's civic, the Co-op's pastoral, the Wake's grim, as in Reborn), the twilight folk's and
  the drifters' in their own; a small set of generated faces for each, as the advisors have.

**Options:** **A. five posts, traits and loyalty (recommended)** · B. admirals and governors only.

## 9. Light espionage (D-74)

- **Agents watch another power,** for credits a turn: each turn they gather intelligence, which
  reveals, as it rises, that power's holdings as if surveyed, its fleets and their strength, its
  technologies and what it researches, and its treasury and keys. What agents learn stays known for
  a while, then goes stale.
- **Agents guard home:** credits spent on guarding lower what others' agents gather.
- **The Watch reports it,** as it reports rumours now, and the diplomacy screen shows what is known
  and how fresh it is.
- **The rivals use it** by character (the Wake and the Moot most).

**Options:** **A. watching and guarding (recommended)** · B. none in 1.0.

## 10. Events and the living sky (D-75)

- **From twelve events to about twenty-six:** the relics' own (a site's dust stirs, a rival's
  excavation is heard, a key sings), the second tone growing louder, a great flare at a flare star
  (that flare stars flare is real; when and how hard is fiction), a leader's crisis, and two for
  each species in its own character. The rest of SCOPE's forty come in M6.
- **The anomalies** keep their place; the three threads of the mystery now count as chapters of it.

**Options:** **A. about fourteen more (recommended)** · B. all forty now.

## 11. The dying star and the unhomed (D-76)

Everything here is **fiction**, labelled so.

- **Arks at the edge.** Between turns 110 and 150 (by the seed), the Watch hears ships at the edge
  of the map: **the unhomed**, survivors of a star beyond the map that is dying. They travel in ark
  fleets, have no homeworld, and ask the powers they reach for a world.
- **The star dies.** Some turns later its death is seen in every sky (as Betelgeuse's was in
  Reborn); the unhomed then have nowhere to go back to.
- **Welcomed, settled, used or fought** (SCOPE §4.11): a power may give them a world it holds or
  claims, and they become a power of their own with one colony, grateful to it; or it may take them
  in as settlers of its own (a colony of their people, with its own needs); or refuse. Refused by
  all, they settle where they can, by force if they must.
- **They play as an AI power** from then on: few, stubborn, and remembering who helped.

**Options:** **A. the arks and the star's death, mid-game (recommended)** · B. the star's death
only, as an event.

## 12. The machine heirs, and the species after them (D-77)

- **The machine heirs** (SCOPE §4.11) come in M5: self-copying machines, made by the Absent and left
  running (§4). Their home is a real white dwarf beyond the frontier (fiction). They need no
  habitable world, only rock and metal; they grow by building copies of themselves, from industry,
  not food; they are cold and logical, regard others for their usefulness, give no gifts, and know
  more about the relics than they say: they begin knowing where two sites lie, and dig fast.
- **They play as an AI power,** designed with enough to be played later (D-08): their own colony
  view buildings, faces (if a machine has one), emblem and words.
- **The under-ice thinkers and the chorus** come after M5. D-12 asked for five or six alien species
  in 1.0; with the heirs and the unhomed there are four.

**Options:** **A. the machine heirs and the unhomed in M5, two more after (recommended)** · B. all
four of SCOPE's remaining species now · C. the unhomed only.

## 13. Pyre (D-78)

Pyre is Reborn's one invented star (fiction): a red supergiant 33 light-years toward Phoenix that
died in Reborn's time and left a black hole, one lane from the real white dwarf GJ 915.

- **A. Off the map, in the story (recommended for M5):** the second tone comes from Pyre's
  direction, and the last revelations point there; GJ 915, on the map, is one of the sites whenever
  it has a world to hold.
- **B. On the map, as in Reborn:** the one invented system, labelled so, with its black hole and
  one dark lane to GJ 915, and the deepest site at it. More pull to the edge, but a new kind of
  system to draw and to explain; best with M6's wider maps.

## 14. The rivals and the unknown

The rivals play the unknown by the same rules as the player:

- **Excavation by character:** the Authority shares a half of its research with its digs, the
  Concord and the Moot a quarter, the Co-op a quarter once it has nothing nearer to settle, the Wake
  none until it holds a key (then it takes them).
- **The keys:** a power that holds three goes for the rest, by digging or by war; every power goes
  to war, or calls the Assembly, against a power building the Answer (§6).
- **Leaders and agents** within what they can pay, by character.

## 15. On the screen

Designed for the phone and the desktop alike (D-04), and tested at both sizes.

- **The map** marks known sites with a small glyph of their kind, and key sites, once known, with
  a key; a filter shows only sites.
- **The system view** shows a site where it lies, on its body or in its belt, with its picture.
- **A site's panel**: its kind, its picture, its chapters (done, under way, to come), who excavates
  it, its guardians, and *Excavate* or *Clear the guardians*.
- **The excavation's share** is set on the research screen, beside the current technology.
- **The chapter's choice** opens as an event does, in the report, with the site's picture.
- **The codex's journal, *The Absent*,** keeps the revelations read so far.
- **The keys** are shown in the standings (who holds how many, as far as is known) and on a key's
  holding; the Answer under way has its own line in every power's report.
- **Leaders** have a screen of their own (hired, posts, traits, offers), and appear wherever their
  post does: on the fleet, the colony, the research and diplomacy screens.
- **Espionage** sits on the diplomacy screen, for each power.
- On a phone, each of these is a sheet; on a desktop, a side panel beside the map.

## 20. Increment 52 in detail: relic sites (written before building it)

**Goals.** §5 and §4's mystery as far as they stand without keys: sites in every game, found by
survey, excavated in chapters paid in research, each chapter ending in a choice and a revelation;
three technologies of Xenology; the rivals digging by character. The keys, the Answer and the
guardians come in increments 53 and 54.

### 20.1 Where the sites are

- **Twelve sites,** chosen from the seed when a game begins and never saved (as the anomalies are),
  among the systems with at least one body, each at least 8 light-years from every capital and at
  most 18 from the nearest (every faction's capital counts, in play or not, so the sites never
  depend on who plays), and at least 3 light-years from one another.
- **GJ 915 is one whenever it has a world to hold** (the white dwarf nearest Pyre, §13, just beyond
  the band; its worlds are generated, and in some games it has none), a *quiet beacon*. Up to five
  more are at white dwarfs (only four lie in the band: Sirius, Procyon, 40 Eridani and Stein 2051,
  so most games have all four), three at brown dwarfs and the rest anywhere, drawn by the seed;
  where a group runs out of systems, the next takes its place.
- **Six kinds,** each twice: *a vault*, *a field of ordered dust*, *an array*, *a hollow moon*, *a
  hall of charts* and *a quiet beacon*. Each has its own picture and three chapters of its own.
  Every site is fiction, and labelled so; nothing is said of the real body or star it lies at.

### 20.2 Finding them

- **A survey finds the site** in the system surveyed, as it finds an anomaly: the report says so,
  and the site is known from then on.
- **Old charts** (§20.6): every site within 12 light-years of one of the power's holdings is known
  too, surveyed or not.

### 20.3 Excavating

- **Who may excavate:** a power that has learnt *Relic studies* and holds a colony or an outpost in
  the site's system, when no other power is excavating it and its third chapter is not done.
  *Excavate* starts it; *Stop* stops it. A site keeps its chapters and its progress toward the next
  whoever excavates it.
- **Paid in research.** A power sets the share of its research that goes to its excavations: none,
  a quarter (the default) or a half. While it has an excavation under way, that share of each turn's
  research goes to its excavations, in equal parts (the odd point to the first by system), and the
  rest to its current technology. With no excavation under way, all of it goes to the technology.
- **A chapter needs** 150, 300 and 500 research points (a fifth less with *Field archaeology*).
- **A chapter done waits for its choice** (§20.4); while it waits, that excavation takes no research.
  Answered, its reward is given, and the next chapter begins. Unanswered after 3 turns, it takes the
  first choice, as an event does.
- **An excavation stops** when its power no longer holds anything in the system (lost, abandoned
  or the power gone), keeping its progress; any power that holds there may take it up.

### 20.4 The chapters and their choices

Each chapter has a few lines of its own and two choices, each saying what it gives:

| Choice | Chapter 1 | Chapter 2 | Chapter 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

- The report says when new offers come: *Three people offer to serve us.*

### 24.4 Loyalty

Each turn, each leader may leave:

- **In debt:** while the power's treasury is below 0, 5 in 100 a turn (10 if Restless).
- **An unhappy colony:** a steward whose colony is in unrest or revolt, 5 in 100 a turn (10 if
  Restless).
- **Lured away:** a rival whose agents know our secrets (75, §23.1) lures one of our leaders, 2 in
  100 a turn for each such rival, unless we keep much guard at home; the leader joins that rival,
  waiting for a post.
- **Fallen:** a commander whose fleet is destroyed in battle falls with it. A fleet merged or
  disbanded sends its commander back to wait.

The report names each: *Steward Ines Barros has left our service: our treasury is empty.*

## 25. Increment 57 in detail: the dying star and the unhomed (written before building it)

**Goals.** §11 (D-76, option A): a people without a world arrives in mid-game, asks the powers for
one, and becomes a power of its own (or joins one) as the powers answer; their star's death is seen
in every sky. Nothing in the rules is theirs alone: they are a faction and a species as data, and
the one new rule is that a faction may *arrive* rather than start. **Out of scope:** playing them
(later, D-08: their advisors' faces, their buildings and their music wait until then, as the
Concord's did); a colony of two peoples (taken in, they join a power's own people); their arks as
ships that fight.

### 25.1 Names and their star (fiction, and what is real)

- **Arcturus** is real: an old orange giant, 36.7 light-years from Sol in Boötes, beyond the map's
  33. That it had worlds and a people, and that it dies in the game, is **fiction**: the real star
  will shine for a very long time yet, and the game says so wherever its death is told.
- **The Unhomed** is the name the powers give the species; their own is a word of warmth that
  translates as *the hearth's people*. Fiction, and like no other work's aliens: tall and stooped,
  with ash-grey skin and large dark eyes made for a dim orange sun, long four-fingered hands, and a
  crown of soft filaments that glow faintly like embers; they wrap themselves in many layers of
  patched ark-cloth against the cold.
- **The Last Hearth** (*the Hearth*): their power once they hold a world. Coral (`#ff8a7a`), and a
  cross beside the colour wherever it is shown (a sixth shape).
- **Their names** are translated pairs of warmth and ash (*Banked Coal*, *Ash Singer*); their envoy
  is *Kindling, who asks*; their speaker *Old Ember, keeper of the hearth*.

### 25.2 The arks arrive

- **When:** on a turn from **110 to 150**, chosen by the seed.
- **Where:** **CE Boötis**, a real red dwarf 32.4 light-years from Sol at the map's edge, the
  nearest star to Arcturus on the map (4.5 ly from it).
- **Every power hears it:** *Ships at CE Boötis, at the edge of the map: arks, very many and very
  old, crossing from Arcturus. They call to every power that can hear in every tongue they have
  learnt from our broadcasts: they ask for a world.* The arks wait at CE Boötis, marked on the map,
  until they are answered.

### 25.3 The ask

Each power in play may answer, once, while the arks wait:

- **Give them a world:** a body in a system where the power holds a colony or an outpost, held by
  no one, that the Unhomed could settle (tolerable, or harsh for want of better). The power names
  it. They settle there as the **Last Hearth**, a power of their own, grateful to the giver.
- **Take them in:** they come to the power's own colonies as its people: **2 million** people to
  each of the three colonies with the most room (as far as it allows), **150 research** from what
  they know, and **−5 approval** at those colonies for 10 turns while they settle (*newcomers*). They
  do not become a power.
- **Refuse.**

As the turn ends, the first answer that settles them takes effect: a world given before a taking-in,
and between two of one kind, the nearer to CE Boötis first. A power that has not answered is taken
to refuse once the arks stop asking.

**The rivals answer by character:** the Authority and the Moot give a world, the Co-op and the Wake
take them in, the Concord refuses (slow to trust); each only when it can (a world to give, a colony
with room), and a few turns after they arrive (by the seed, 1 to 4).

### 25.4 The star dies

**12 turns** after the arks arrive, Arcturus dies, in every power's report: *Arcturus has thrown off
its outer layers in a single night: a new light in Boötes, bright enough to read by. The Unhomed
have nowhere to go back to.* With the note: *Arcturus is real, an old giant 37 light-years away; its
death is the game's fiction.* Every power studies it: **+40 research**.

If the arks are still waiting, they stop asking and **take a world by force**: the free body the
Unhomed could best settle within 10 ly of CE Boötis (the nearest on a tie), or, failing that, the
nearest free body they could settle at all. They remember each power that refused them.

### 25.5 The Last Hearth, a power in mid-game

- **A new power** is added to the game as a rival, with its capital colony of **3 million**
  people on the world given or taken, a shipyard and a lane station, a survey ship and a colony
  ship, 100 credits, a small stockpile, and every technology that **every** power in play knows
  (what all know, they have learnt from listening); no lanes of its own yet.
- **It knows every power,** in talk: they have heard each other's tongues for 12 turns.
- **It remembers:** **+30** for the power that gave it a world (*Gave us a home*), **−15** for each
  power that refused it (*Refused us a home*), fading a point every **12** turns (slow to forget).
- **As a rival:** few and stubborn. Its capital and colonies grow; it settles the nearest world;

  Lawful. Leaders: a steward, an ambassador, a scholar. No excavations of its own at first (a
  quarter of its research once it has one, like the Concord).
- **The rules treat it as any power:** contact, treaties, the Assembly, war, score and victory.

### 25.6 On the screen
